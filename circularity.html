Circular economy

Scrap in, steel out, nothing wasted.

Traditional manufacturing runs take-make-dispose. A circular approach keeps resources in use for as long as possible, extracting maximum value while in use, then recovering and regenerating materials at end of life.

Why it matters: steel made from recycled scrap in an EAF uses significantly less energy than primary steelmaking from ore — and when that furnace runs on renewable power, emissions approach near-zero.

The scrap-first route

Why scrap recycling is the fastest lever we have.

Less Energy

Producing steel from recycled scrap in an EAF uses significantly less energy than primary steelmaking from iron ore.

Near-Zero Potential

EAFs powered by renewable energy can achieve near-zero emissions — the destination our roadmap is built toward.

Resource Conservation

Prioritising scrap-based production conserves natural resources and minimises waste, not just emissions.

Beyond the melt shop

Fly Ash By-Products

Circularity doesn't stop at scrap. Our Fly Ash By-Products division repurposes combustion residue from our power operations into usable material rather than sending it to landfill — extending the same circular logic across the plant.

This is a small but telling example of how sustainability gets built into operations rather than bolted on afterward: every by-product is a question of "what can this become," not "where does this go."

Carbon and alloy steel manufacturer in Cuttack, Odisha — supplying special steel, ferro alloys and wire rods to India's auto, defence, energy and rail industries since 1979.
